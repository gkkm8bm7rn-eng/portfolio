02 · Strategic problem

AI alone was not a strong enough USP

Competitor research showed that AI personalization, variables, inbox controls, and prospect-specific messaging already appear across the category. Positioning the page around “we have AI” would not create a credible differentiation angle.

Too generic

“AI personalization”

A category feature rather than a defensible reason to choose the product.

Chosen direction

Depth + control

Prospect-specific AI sequences combined with deterministic templates, fallbacks, and review before sending.

The resulting message became: personalization at scale + control over what gets sent.

06 · Build

Turning the specification into a real interface

The coded version is deliberately lightweight and dependency-free. The goal was not to demonstrate framework complexity; it was to turn the approved marketing and visual logic into a working, reviewable page.

HTML

Semantic page structure

One H1, clear H2 hierarchy, native details/summary FAQ, accessible navigation.

CSS

Responsive product-led visual system

Desktop, tablet, and mobile layouts with schematic product UI instead of decorative stock imagery.

JS

Only where interaction helps

Mobile navigation, reveal behavior, and a Local / Cloud pricing switch.

QA

Claims stay constrained

No invented conversion metrics, absolute safety promises, or unsupported AI autonomy.

07 · AI-assisted workflow

AI as a production system, not a one-shot generator

AI supported research organization, comparison, drafting, code generation, and repeated QA passes. The working method was iterative: every stage was checked against the brief, evidence, and the previous approved stage before moving forward.

Brief→Structured task→Draft→Fact check→QA→Refinement

The point of the workflow is not “AI made a page.” It is using AI to compress production time while keeping scope, evidence, and final decisions under human control.
